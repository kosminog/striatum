# Marketing formats

Skeletons, not templates. Every section should answer an objection the reader has
at that point in the scroll.

## Landing page

1. **Hero**: headline (promise), sub-headline (how, in one line), primary CTA,
   one proof element (logo strip, number, quote).
2. **The problem, in their words**: two or three sentences the reader would nod at.
3. **How it works**: three steps, each with a concrete noun and a verb.
4. **Proof**: one detailed customer story beats six logos. Numbers with sources.
5. **Objections**: pricing, security, migration, "what if it does not fit us".
6. **Final CTA**: same promise, same button, plus what happens after they click.

Length: enough to answer the objections, no more. Cold traffic needs more; a
retargeted visitor needs less.

## Launch announcement / product blog post

1. Headline: what is new and the outcome ("Scheduled exports: your data where you
   need it, every morning").
2. First paragraph: who it is for and what they can now do. No history.
3. One screenshot or example with a real value.
4. How to get it: two lines, with a link.
5. What is next, in one sentence, only if it is committed.

Under 400 words. Everything else goes in the docs.

## Email campaign

- Subject: under 45 characters, the promise or the news, no clickbait.
- Preheader: completes the subject, does not repeat it.
- Body: one idea, under 150 words, one CTA button, plain-text fallback reads well.
- Sender: a person's name if a person will reply.
- Footer: legal, unsubscribe, physical address where required.

## Ad (search, social, display)

- Headline: promise plus the reader's word for the problem.
- Body: one proof point.
- CTA: outcome-phrased.
- Match the landing page headline so the click feels continuous.

## Social post

- First line does the whole job; the rest is optional.
- One idea, one link, no hashtag stacks.
- Platform tone shifts, brand voice does not.
- If it is a thread, each post stands alone.

## App store listing

- First 80 characters of the description are visible without tapping: promise there.
- Feature bullets as outcomes ("Find any receipt in two taps").
- Screenshots with captions carry more than the text.
- Keywords in prose, not lists.

## Tagline / positioning statement

Positioning (internal): For [reader] who [situation], [Name] is the [category] that
[single differentiator], unlike [alternative] which [limitation].

Tagline (external): under six words, one idea, survives without the logo, does not
contain the category name. Generate thirty, keep three, test.
