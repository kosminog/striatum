# Message types

Each skeleton is the order of sentences. Adjust length to the relationship.

## Follow-up / chase

1. The ask, with the date it was originally sent and the new deadline.
2. What happens if yes; what happens if no or if changes are needed.
3. One line making it easy: "A one-word reply is fine."

Never "just checking in". Say what you need.

## Status update

Subject: `[Project] status — <date>: <one-word state>` (on track / at risk / blocked).

1. State in one line.
2. Done since last update (three bullets max).
3. Next (three bullets max), each with an owner and a date.
4. Risks or blockers, each with the decision or help needed and from whom.

No narrative. If it is all green, keep it to five lines.

## Decline

1. The decision, first sentence, without "unfortunately".
2. One honest reason, briefly. Do not stack reasons.
3. What you can do instead, if anything real.
4. Warm close that does not reopen the question.

## Delay / missed deadline

1. The new date and what it affects, first line.
2. Why, in one sentence, owned in the active voice.
3. What you are doing to prevent a further slip.
4. What you need from them, if anything.

Send it the moment you know, not the day it was due.

## Dispute (invoice, scope, quality)

1. The specific item and the specific discrepancy, with numbers and references.
2. What you believe was agreed, citing the document or message.
3. The resolution you propose.
4. The date by which you would like a reply.

Neutral tone throughout. This one will be forwarded.

## Apology

1. What happened, plainly, and its effect on them.
2. "I'm sorry" or "we're sorry", once, attached to the specific thing.
3. What has been done and what will be done, with dates.
4. How to reach you if it is not resolved.

No "if this caused any inconvenience". It did; that is why you are writing.

## Introduction

Ask both parties before connecting them. Then:

1. One line each on who the other is and why they should talk.
2. The specific thing you think they can do for each other.
3. Move yourself to BCC in the first reply.

## Proposal / quote

1. What you understood the need to be, in their words (one paragraph).
2. What you propose, as outcomes, then deliverables.
3. Price, terms, timeline, in a table.
4. What is not included.
5. Next step and expiry date of the quote.

## Cold outreach

1. Why them specifically, one line that proves you looked.
2. The one thing you can do for them, in their terms.
3. A small, easy ask (a 15-minute call, a yes/no).
4. Under 90 words. No attachments. No "I hope this finds you well".

## Chat (Slack, Teams)

- Lead with the ask; do not send "hi" and wait.
- One message, not six fragments. Use a thread for detail.
- Tag only people who must act. State the deadline in the message, not the reminder.
- If it needs more than three exchanges, propose a call.
