# Declining a scope request (invented names, for illustration only)

**Reader:** Priya, a client's product manager, mid-project.
**Outcome:** she drops the request from this phase without feeling brushed off.
**Temperature:** warm; the relationship is good and we want the next phase.
**Record:** yes, this may be quoted in a scope discussion later.

## Before

> Subject: Re: Re: Re: Dashboard idea
>
> Hi Priya,
>
> Thanks so much for the suggestion, it's a really interesting idea and we can
> definitely see the value! Unfortunately, as you know, the timeline is quite
> tight at the moment and the team is pretty stretched, and there are also some
> technical considerations around the data model that would need looking into,
> so it might be tricky to fit in right now. But let's definitely keep it on the
> radar and maybe revisit it at some point! Hope that makes sense.

## After

> Subject: Dashboard filters: scheduling for phase 2
>
> Hi Priya,
>
> We won't add the dashboard filters to this phase. Doing so would push the
> 14 November launch by about two weeks, and the launch date matters more to
> your team than the filters do right now.
>
> I've written the request up as the first item for phase 2, with a two-day
> estimate. If that priority changes, tell me and we'll re-plan.
>
> Thanks for flagging it. It's a good addition.
>
> Alex

## What changed

- The decision is the first sentence, without "unfortunately" (the ask or the
  news goes first).
- Three stacked, hedged reasons became one honest one with a date and a number
  (only the context the reader needs).
- "Keep it on the radar" became a concrete place and estimate, and the subject
  line is searchable (closing action: who, what, by when).
